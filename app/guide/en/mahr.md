Mahr is one of the things people ask about most before a nikah, and talk about least openly. This article sets out what the Qur'an and Sunnah say about mahr, how the Hanafi school reads it, and where the schools differ.

## What mahr is

Mahr (also mehr or sadaq) is the marriage gift a husband gives his wife. The Qur'an calls it a gift given willingly:

> And give the women their bridal gifts as a free gift. But if they give up willingly to you anything of it, then take it in satisfaction and ease.
> — An-Nisa, 4:4, meaning

Elsewhere the Qur'an says that women are to be given their mahr "as an obligation" (An-Nisa, 4:24). This is why scholars describe mahr as **an obligation on the husband**, not a present that depends on his mood or on custom.

## Mahr belongs to the wife

Mahr is not a price, and it does not belong to her father or her family. It is hers to keep, spend or give away. The same verse (4:4) allows the husband to take from it only what she herself gives up willingly, so she may forgive all or part of it, but never under pressure. The Qur'an also forbids a husband from taking back what he gave, even if it was "a heap of gold" (An-Nisa, 4:20).

## Prompt and deferred mahr

Mahr can be given all at once or split in two:

- **Prompt mahr** (mu'ajjal, also called muqaddam) is the part given straight away, at the nikah or soon after it.
- **Deferred mahr** (mu'akhkhar) is the part postponed. It remains a debt the husband owes his wife. In the Hanafi school, if no date was set, it falls due at the latest on divorce or on the death of either spouse.

Deferred mahr is not a formality. If the husband dies, it is paid as a debt from his estate before the inheritance is divided, because the Qur'an places the division of inheritance "after any bequest or debt" (An-Nisa, 4:11–12).

## How much: the minimum and the maximum

**The minimum.** In the Hanafi school, mahr may not be less than ten silver dirhams. This position is set out in Hanafi works such as *Al-Hidaya* (al-Marghinani), and draws on a narration recorded by ad-Daraqutni and al-Bayhaqi. The other schools differ here: the Maliki school has its own, lower minimum, and the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools set no minimum at all, accepting anything that has value.

The value of ten dirhams today is worked out from the price of silver, so it changes. That is why we do not give a figure here: ask the imam of your mosque or your local Islamic centre for the current minimum.

**The maximum.** No upper limit is set; scholars cite the "heap of gold" verse (4:20) as evidence.

## Moderation

Allowed is not the same as desirable. The Sunnah points towards moderation:

> The most blessed marriage is the one with the least burden.
> — Ahmad, Musnad, meaning

Abu Dawud records a shorter narration: the best marriage is the easiest one. Umar, may Allah be pleased with him, warned against excess in mahr, and said that the Prophet, peace be upon him, did not give any of his wives or daughters more than twelve uqiyah (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i). Aisha, may Allah be pleased with her, said the mahr of the Prophet's wives was five hundred dirhams (Muslim).

The size of the mahr is not a measure of a woman's worth. An excessive mahr easily delays the nikah or starts the marriage in debt; the measure is what the husband can give and what she is happy with.

## What can be given as mahr

The basic rule across the schools is that mahr is something of value that can be owned:

- **Money**, in any currency.
- **Gold or silver**, jewellery or another valuable item.
- **Property or other assets**, as long as it is clear what is being given.

There is a well-known hadith about a man who had nothing to give. The Prophet told him to look for even an iron ring, and when he could not find that either, married him "for what you know of the Qur'an" (Bukhari, Muslim). On the basis of this hadith the Shafi'i school accepts teaching the Qur'an as mahr. The Hanafi school requires mahr to be property, so in Hanafi practice it is set in money or valuables. If you are thinking of a mahr that is neither money nor an item, ask your imam before the nikah.

An engagement ring or other present is not mahr in itself; if it is meant as mahr, say so at the nikah.

## How it is agreed and named at the nikah

Mahr is agreed before the nikah, between the two of them, with their families if they wish: not haggling over a price, but a conversation about what is fair and possible, including how much is prompt and how much deferred.

At the nikah the imam usually asks what the mahr is and mentions it before the witnesses, along with what is prompt and what is deferred. Ask whether it will also be written down.

If the mahr is not mentioned at the nikah, the nikah is still valid, and the wife is entitled to the mahr usually given to women like her (mahr al-mithl). This also follows from the Qur'an, which speaks of divorce before a mahr has been fixed (Al-Baqarah, 2:236). If a divorce happens before the marriage is consummated and a mahr was fixed, she is owed half of it (Al-Baqarah, 2:237).

## In short

- Mahr is the husband's obligation and the wife's right (An-Nisa, 4:4 and 4:24); it belongs to her alone.
- It can be prompt or deferred; the deferred part is a debt.
- The Hanafi minimum is ten silver dirhams; ask your imam for the current amount.
- The Sunnah recommends moderation.
- Agree it in good time and have it stated clearly at the nikah.
