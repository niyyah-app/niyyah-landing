Nikah is a contract, and one the Qur'an calls "a firm commitment" (An-Nisa, 4:21). Like any contract it has its pillars and conditions: what must be present for it to be valid, what is Sunnah, and what is simply custom. This article goes through them in order, following the Hanafi school, which is followed in Bosnia and Herzegovina, and noting where the other schools differ.

## The pillar of nikah: offer and acceptance

The core of a nikah is offer (ijab) and acceptance (qabul): one side clearly offers marriage, the other clearly accepts it, in the same sitting. In practice the imam usually asks the bride and groom in front of the witnesses, and they state their consent. The words should be clear and in the past or present tense ("I accept", "I agree"), not a promise about the future.

## Conditions for a valid nikah

**1. Both are able to marry.** They are adult and of sound mind, and there is no impediment between them: they are not mahram to each other (see [Who is a mahram](/en/guide/who-is-a-mahram)), and the woman is not married or in her iddah (the waiting period after divorce or her husband's death).

**2. The woman's consent.** A marriage without her will is not a marriage. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said that a woman who has been married before is consulted, and a virgin is asked for her permission (Bukhari, Muslim). No one, not even her father, may marry her off against her will.

**3. Two witnesses.** In the Hanafi school these are two men, or one man and two women. They should be Muslim, adult and of sound mind, and they should hear the offer and acceptance together. Witnesses are what make a nikah public; a secret marriage is not what Islam asks for.

## The wali

The wali is the woman's representative when the marriage is contracted: her father, and if he is absent, her grandfather, son, brother, and so on down the line of male relatives on her father's side.

This is where the schools differ:

- **The Hanafi school:** an adult woman of sound mind may contract her own marriage, and a wali is not a condition of validity. It is still recommended that the wali be involved, and he has the right to object if she marries someone who is not her equal (kafa'ah).
- **The Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali schools:** a wali is a condition. They rely on the hadith "there is no marriage without a wali" (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi).

In practice, in Bosnia as elsewhere, the wali is almost always part of the nikah — not as someone who decides for her, but as family standing beside her.

## Mahr

Mahr is the marriage gift the husband gives his wife. It belongs to her, not to her family, and she uses it as she wishes. The Qur'an says that women are to be given their bridal gifts as a gift from the heart (An-Nisa, 4:4, meaning).

- **Mahr is the husband's obligation**, but if it is not specified at the nikah, the nikah is still valid, and the wife is entitled to the mahr that women like her receive.
- **It can be money, gold or something else of benefit.** The Prophet told a man who had nothing to look for even an iron ring (Bukhari, Muslim), and when that same man could not find even that, his mahr was to teach his wife the surahs of the Qur'an he knew.
- **Part can be given straight away and part deferred.** The deferred part remains a debt the husband owes.
- **It should not be excessive.** The size of the mahr is not a measure of the marriage, and an excessive mahr easily becomes a burden instead of a gift.

It is good to talk about mahr in good time, calmly and honestly, not at the last minute in front of the imam. More in [Mahr in Islam](/en/guide/mahr), and for the wider conversation, [Questions to ask before marriage](/en/guide/questions-to-ask-before-marriage).

## What is Sunnah

- **The khutbah of nikah** — a short address by the imam before the offer and acceptance.
- **Announcing the marriage** — so that people know the two are husband and wife.
- **Walimah** — the wedding meal. The Prophet told one of his companions: hold a walimah, even if only with one sheep (Bukhari, Muslim).
- **The du'a for the couple** — in meaning: "May Allah bless you, and send His blessing upon you, and join you together in good" (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi).

## The order of the wedding in Bosnia and Herzegovina

In Bosnia and Herzegovina, the Islamic Community (Islamska zajednica) requires the Islamic nikah to take place after the civil marriage at the municipality. So the usual order there is:

1. The civil marriage before the registrar.
2. The nikah with the imam, with the marriage certificate and the other documents the local medžlis asks for.
3. The walimah and wedding celebration, as means and custom allow.

The exact list of documents and dates in BiH is something to check with the local medžlis or the imam of the congregation. Outside Bosnia the rules depend on the country you live in; the imam of your mosque will know what applies there.

## In short

- A nikah is made by offer and acceptance, the woman's consent and two witnesses.
- In the Hanafi school a wali is not a condition of validity but is recommended; in the other three schools he is a condition.
- Mahr is the husband's obligation and the wife's right; it does not have to be large.
- In Bosnia and Herzegovina the nikah comes after the civil marriage.

This article is an overview, not a fatwa. For anything specific to your situation — a previous marriage, different countries, family circumstances — speak with an imam before the nikah.
