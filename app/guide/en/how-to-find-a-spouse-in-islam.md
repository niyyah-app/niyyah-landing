Looking for a spouse is one of the few big decisions a person makes with the heart and the mind at the same time. Islam does not offer a formula for finding "the one", but it gives clear guidance: what intention to start with, what to look for, how to conduct yourself while searching, and how to hand the decision over to Allah.

## Start with the intention

The first hadith in Bukhari's collection says that deeds are judged by intentions, and every person will have what they intended (Bukhari, Muslim). That same hadith mentions, as an example, the one who set out for the sake of a woman he wished to marry.

The intention for marriage is more than "I want to get married". It is: I want a marriage that brings me closer to Allah, in which I will protect and be protected, and in which we will raise our children in faith. With that intention, the search itself becomes an act of worship rather than a race.

The word *niyyah*, intention, is the name of this app for exactly that reason.

## What to look for

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said that a woman is married for four things: her wealth, her lineage, her beauty and her religion — and advised choosing the one who has faith (Bukhari, Muslim). To the families of young women he said:

> When someone whose religion and character you are pleased with comes to you, marry your daughter to him. If you do not, there will be trial on earth and great corruption.
> — Tirmidhi

Faith and character, then, are the measure for both. The rest is not forbidden to consider — attraction is part of marriage, which is why the Prophet advised seeing a prospective spouse before proposing. But the rest comes after.

How to recognise faith and character in someone you are only getting to know:

- **How they speak about their parents.** How someone treats their parents is often the best indicator of how they will treat a spouse.
- **How they speak about others.** Former friends, colleagues, people they do not like.
- **Whether they are consistent.** Whether what they say about faith shows in how they live.
- **Whether they are honest when it is hard.** How they answer a question that has no pleasant answer.
- **How they behave towards a third person** — a waiter, a younger sibling, a mahram who is present.

## Compatibility (kafa'ah)

Scholars speak of kafa'ah — the idea that spouses should be each other's "equals". The most important part of this is equality in religion, but the things that will shape a shared life also count: education, way of life, expectations. It is not that one is worth more than the other, but that a house is easier to build when the foundations match. So it is wise to ask what matters early; see [Questions to ask before marriage](/en/guide/questions-to-ask-before-marriage).

## Where to look

Throughout history Muslims have met through someone: family, relatives, the mosque, friends. That still works today, but the circle is often small, especially for Muslims living as a minority, and the pressure can be heavy.

That is why many people today also look through apps. This can be fine, provided the limits are clear: the intention of marriage, no seclusion, and family beside you. More on this in [Is it halal to look for a spouse through an app?](/en/guide/is-a-muslim-marriage-app-halal)

Whichever route you choose, involve your family early. For sisters especially, a [mahram](/en/guide/who-is-a-mahram) who is present from the start is a protection, not a hindrance.

## Istikhara

When the time comes to decide, the Prophet taught his companions istikhara "as he taught them a surah of the Qur'an" (Bukhari). Istikhara means asking Allah to choose what is good.

How it is prayed:

1. Pray two rak'ahs of voluntary prayer, outside the times when prayer is disliked.
2. After the prayer, recite the du'a of istikhara. In meaning it says: O Allah, if You know this matter is good for my religion, my life and my end, decree it for me and make it easy; and if You know it is bad, turn it away from me and me from it, and decree good for me wherever it may be.
3. Where the du'a mentions "this matter", you have in mind the specific decision — marriage to that person.

Istikhara is not a search for a dream or a sign. Scholars say the answer shows in how things unfold afterwards: they become easier, or the door closes. Nor does istikhara replace reason and advice: alongside it, you consult your family and people you trust.

## Du'a

The Qur'an teaches a du'a for a good spouse:

> Our Lord, grant us from among our spouses and offspring comfort to our eyes, and make us leaders for the righteous.
> — Al-Furqan, 25:74

Men and women alike make this du'a, at any time, and especially in sujud and in the last third of the night.

## Patience

Some people find someone quickly; for others it takes years. Neither is a measure of a person's worth. Your part is to search with the right intention, to be honest, to guard yourself and to make du'a. The outcome is with Allah.

## In short

- Start with the intention: marriage for the sake of Allah, not as an escape or under pressure.
- Look at faith and character first; the rest comes after.
- Involve your family early, and keep the limits while you get to know each other.
- Ask what matters in good time, pray istikhara, and make du'a.
