Search for a Muslim marriage app and you will find many that look alike: a logo with a crescent, a promise of "halal", a photo of a happy couple. The words are easy to copy. What is harder to copy is how the app actually behaves once you are inside it. These are the things worth checking before you give any app your name, your photos and your time.

## 1. Is it built for marriage, or for dating?

An app made for marriage asks about what matters before the nikah: prayer, madhhab, whether you want children, whether you would move, when you hope to marry. An app made for dating asks for photos and a clever line. Look at the profile it asks you to fill in. It tells you what the app thinks you are there for.

**In Niyyah:** a profile carries prayer, madhhab, marriage plans, children and relocation, and Discover shows only the opposite gender.

## 2. Who can write to you, and when?

If anyone can message anyone, the inbox fills up with people you never chose, and the conversations that matter get lost. A good Muslim marriage app lets a conversation begin only when both people want it.

**In Niyyah:** a conversation opens only when you both like each other. Someone can also send a like with a short message attached; you read it for free, but a conversation still opens only if you like them back.

## 3. Can your family be part of it?

For many families, getting to know someone for marriage has always happened with family nearby. On a phone, that disappears unless the app makes room for it. Ask whether a father, brother or guardian can follow the conversation, and how.

**In Niyyah:** a sister can turn on "Require a mahram" and invite up to three people. A person on our team first checks each mahram's identity. The mahram approves each new conversation, reads it in the Mahram portal, and can write only if she or the person she is talking to allows it. How it works step by step is in [Family in the conversation](/en/guide/family-and-mahram-in-the-app).

## 4. How does it know people are real?

Anyone can write a profile and borrow a photo. Ask what the app does about it: is there a face check, and is anything checked by a person, or only by a machine?

**In Niyyah:** a short live selfie is compared with the profile photos. A clear match is approved straight away, and anything unclear is decided by a person from our team. Profiles that pass carry a verified badge. More in [Profile verification](/en/guide/profile-verification).

## 5. Do you control who sees your photos?

Many sisters are uncomfortable with their face being shown to every stranger who opens the app. An app that understands this lets you choose.

**In Niyyah:** your photos can be visible, blurred, or hidden. A match does not open them on its own; you open them to one person at a time.

## 6. Can you ask what matters before the first message?

Some questions decide everything: prayer, children, where you will live. It saves both people time to know the answers before a conversation even begins.

**In Niyyah:** you can set up to three questions of your own. After a match, the other person answers them first, and you decide whether the conversation opens.

## 7. Who handles reports?

Every app says it is safe. Ask who reads a report when you send one, what they can do about it, and whether a banned person can simply come back with a new email address.

**In Niyyah:** reports are read by real moderators who can suspend and ban profiles. Blocking works both ways, and a banned account cannot simply return with a new email.

## 8. What is free, and what does it really cost?

Read the price list before you sign up, not after. Watch for subscriptions that renew on their own, and for safety features that are only available to people who pay.

**In Niyyah:** everything that keeps you safe is free: mahram, questions before chat, locked conversations and moderation, plus 30 profiles and 20 likes a day and unlimited conversation with your matches. Extras are paid in coins, which you also earn for free every day. There is no subscription, so there is nothing to cancel. The full list is in the [FAQ on the Niyyah page](/en#pitanja).

## 9. What happens to your data?

You are telling the app about your faith, your family and your plans. Ask who sees that, who it is shared with, and whether your exact location is ever shown.

**In Niyyah:** personal data is not sold or given to advertisers. Your exact location is never shown, and hidden data in photos, such as GPS location, is removed on upload. Every service the app uses is named in the [privacy policy](/en/privacy).

## 10. Who is behind it?

An app that asks for your trust should tell you who runs it and how to reach them. A company name, an address and a support email are the minimum.

**In Niyyah:** the app is run by Puzzles IT OÜ, a company registered in Estonia and responsible for your data under European data protection law. You can reach the team at support@niyyahmarriage.com.

## What no app can do for you

An app can keep the boundaries, check who people are and bring your family into the conversation. It cannot know your intention, and it cannot replace the people who know you. Pray istikhara, talk to your family, and if you are unsure whether a way of looking for a spouse is right, ask an imam you trust. If you want to read more about that question, start with [Is it halal to look for a spouse through an app?](/en/guide/is-a-muslim-marriage-app-halal)
