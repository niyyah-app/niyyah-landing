This article is for fathers, mothers and guardians, and for the brother or uncle the family leans on. When a daughter or son starts looking for a spouse, a parent is caught between two concerns: that the child should not be alone in something so important, and that the parent should not be the one deciding for them. Islam gives a clear framework for both.

## The decision is theirs

A marriage without consent is not a marriage. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said that a woman who has been married before is consulted, and a virgin is asked for her permission (Bukhari, Muslim). When a young woman came to him and said her father had married her off against her will, the Prophet gave her the right to annul that marriage (Abu Dawud).

That does not mean a parent has no role. It means the parent's role is advice, protection and du'a — not the choice itself.

## What a parent can and should do

**Be there from the start.** A child who knows they can come to a parent with "I have met someone" without fear will come sooner. A child who expects an interrogation will hide it.

**Ask around.** Families have always made enquiries about a prospective spouse: what he is like, what his family is like, how people remember him. That is still wise today, as long as it is done with measure and without backbiting.

**Look at what matters.** The Prophet told families: when someone whose religion and character you are pleased with comes to you, marry your daughter to him (Tirmidhi). Faith and character are the measure. Wealth, background and "what will people say" are not reasons to turn down a good person.

**Do not delay without reason.** Putting off a marriage because of the wedding that "has to" be held, because of a flat, or because "there is plenty of time" often does more harm than good.

**Make du'a.** The du'a of a parent for their child is answered (Tirmidhi). It may be the greatest help a parent can give.

## What is better avoided

- **Pressure about age.** "All your friends are married" does not help anyone find a good spouse; it only helps them marry faster.
- **Comparing them with other children** or with "better prospects".
- **Refusing without explanation.** If you have a serious objection, state it clearly and calmly, with your reason.
- **Taking over the conversation.** A parent who talks in the child's place learns what the prospective spouse is like with the parent, not with the child.

## If your child is looking through an app

Many parents' first reaction is fear, and that is understandable: what people know about dating apps is not pretty. But the question is not where your child met someone, but how that getting-to-know is conducted. More in [Is it halal to look for a spouse through an app?](/en/guide/is-a-muslim-marriage-app-halal)

The biggest worry is usually that a daughter is alone in conversations with strangers. Islam answered this long ago: the mahram. When a mahram is present there is no seclusion, and the conversation can be open. More in [Who is a mahram](/en/guide/who-is-a-mahram).

## The mahram in Niyyah: how it looks for a parent

In the Niyyah app, a daughter can turn on "Require a mahram" in her settings and invite up to three people: her father, brother, paternal or maternal uncle, guardian, or another mahram.

- The invitation arrives by email, and the mahram accepts it in the app.
- In the **Mahram portal** you see her conversations and matches.
- You only read: you cannot write from the portal, and you do not use the app to meet anyone.
- One mahram can look out for more than one person, for example a father for two daughters.
- The feature is available on women's profiles.

This means you know who she is talking to and how, with nothing hidden — and she is not living with someone constantly looking over her shoulder at her phone. For the man, it is a clear sign that she is serious and that her family is involved. More on the [mahram section of the Niyyah page](/en#mahrem), and step by step in [Family in the conversation](/en/guide/family-and-mahram-in-the-app).

If you are the mahram, the advice is simple: read with trust, not suspicion. Talk with her about what you see, and leave her room to decide for herself.

## And sons?

The Mahram portal is for daughters, because in Islam the mahram's role is to accompany a woman. But a son needs a parent too: someone who will ask him what intention he is searching with, remind him to keep the limits and to guard other people's honour, and be there when the time comes for the families to meet. [Questions to ask before marriage](/en/guide/questions-to-ask-before-marriage) can be a good start to that conversation.

## In short

- The decision belongs to your child; a parent's role is advice, protection and du'a.
- Faith and character are the measure, not wealth or other people's opinions.
- Be there early, ask around with measure, and do not delay without reason.
- If your daughter is looking through an app, a mahram is a way to stand beside her without standing over her.
