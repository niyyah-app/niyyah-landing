The question gets asked from two directions. Some ask because they want to marry and the circle of people they know is small — especially far from home. Others ask because they have seen what ordinary dating apps look like and do not want their daughter or sister anywhere near one. Both questions are fair, and both deserve an answer that starts not with "yes" or "no", but with what Islam actually asks.

## What Islam asks of two people getting to know each other for marriage

Marriage in Islam is a sunnah and a protection, and looking for a spouse with the right intention is a good deed. The Prophet, peace be upon him, told the young: whoever among you is able to marry should marry, for it lowers the gaze and guards chastity (Bukhari, Muslim). The Qur'an describes marriage like this:

> And among His signs is that He created for you, from among yourselves, spouses so that you may find tranquillity in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy.
> — Ar-Rum, 30:21

Getting to know someone before marriage is not forbidden. The Prophet advised a man who wanted to propose to see the woman first, "for that is more likely to bring love between you" (Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i). What Islam asks for are limits around that getting-to-know:

- **The intention of marriage.** You are meeting to find out whether you can build a life together, not for entertainment.
- **No seclusion.** A man and a woman who are not mahram do not remain alone together (Bukhari, Muslim). More in [Who is a mahram](/en/guide/who-is-a-mahram).
- **Guarding the gaze and the speech.** The Qur'an asks believing men and women to lower their gaze (An-Nur, 24:30–31) and not to speak in a way that invites desire (Al-Ahzab, 33:32).
- **Family is not shut out.** Through the centuries, family has been part of the path to marriage: they meet, ask around, and in the end give their blessing.

## The place is not the problem. The way is.

People have always been introduced through someone: relatives, neighbours, the local imam, a colleague. An app is, in that sense, just another place where two people can hear of each other. The question is not whether that place is a phone, but what happens there.

And this is where ordinary dating apps go wrong — by design. They are built for scrolling, because every minute in the app counts as success. So they have:

- endless cards of people you look at just to look
- messages from anyone, unfiltered, often unpleasant
- conversations nobody else sees and you would not show your parents
- people with no clear intention alongside those who have one

None of this is a side issue a user can get around with a strong conscience. It is the design itself. So whether an app is halal depends on what it allows and what it does not.

## What has to be in place for it to stay within the limits

If an app is meant to serve marriage, its limits should not depend on how strong someone happens to feel that day. They should be built in:

1. **Only the opposite gender, and only with marriage in mind.** No browsing for the sake of browsing.
2. **No unwanted messages.** A conversation opens only when both people want it.
3. **Family can be present.** A mahram can see the conversation, as he would be in the room.
4. **What matters is asked early.** Faith, children, moving — before a month of messaging.
5. **A community someone looks after.** Reports read by people, and consequences for those who break the rules.

## How Niyyah does it

Niyyah is built around those five points, and they are not settings someone can switch off:

- **Discover shows only the opposite gender.** That is a server rule, not a filter.
- **Messages open only on mutual interest.** Until you both like each other, the conversation is locked. From Discover you can send one short introductory message, but it waits and is delivered only once you match.
- **A mahram in the conversation.** A sister can invite up to three people — her father, brother, uncle or guardian — who see her conversations in the Mahram portal but cannot write.
- **Questions before chatting.** You set up to three questions of your own; after a match, the other person answers them first, and you decide whether the conversation opens. More in [Questions to ask before marriage](/en/guide/questions-to-ask-before-marriage).
- **A moderated community.** Reports are read by real moderators, community posts are reviewed before they appear, and blocking works both ways.

All of this is free. We do not charge for what keeps it halal.

## What we cannot say

We do not issue fatwas, and we do not claim that any institution has approved Niyyah. What we can show are the limits the app holds. The intention you bring to it is yours — as with any other way of meeting someone.

If you have doubts about your own situation, ask an imam you trust. It helps if he knows how the app works: show him what the Mahram portal looks like and how a conversation opens, and let him judge what he sees.

## Short and practical

If you decide to look for a spouse through an app, this helps keep it what it should be:

- Start with your intention, and renew it when you feel it slipping.
- Tell your family early, not once everything is decided.
- If you are a sister, involve a mahram from the first conversation.
- Ask what matters to you at the start, not after three weeks.
- Do not drag it out. When it is clear it is not right, end it kindly. When it is, involve both families and move towards the nikah.
- Pray istikhara before you decide.
