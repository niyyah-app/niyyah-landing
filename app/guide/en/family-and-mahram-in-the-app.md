In many families, getting to know someone for marriage has always happened with the family nearby: in the living room, with a father or brother present, with nothing hidden. When the conversation moves to a phone, that presence disappears unless something brings it back. In Niyyah, that something is the mahram feature. This text describes how it works in the app. Who counts as a mahram, and why, is explained in [Who is a mahram](/en/guide/who-is-a-mahram).

## Who can be invited

A sister turns on **"Require a mahram"** in settings and invites up to three people. When inviting, she says who each one is to her: father, brother, uncle, guardian, or another mahram. The app also allows a guardian, because not every sister has a father or brother beside her.

The invitation arrives by email. The mahram accepts it in the app, with his own Niyyah account. An invitation stays open for 14 days; after that it opens nothing, and she can send a new one. One mahram can look after more than one person, for example a father for two daughters.

The feature is available on women's profiles, and it is free.

## First, his identity

Before he sees anything, a person from our team checks who he is: he sends a photo of an identity document (ID card, passport or driving licence) and a selfie. Until that check passes, he sees only the verification screen: no profiles, no conversations, no decisions. The document and the selfie exist only while they wait for review, and are deleted as soon as a decision is made.

## What the mahram sees and decides

When she and someone else like each other, a match is made. If she has asked for a mahram:

1. **The conversation waits for him.** Messages do not go anywhere until at least one of her mahrams can read the conversation. If she has not invited anyone yet, the app asks her to do so first.
2. **He looks and decides.** In the **Mahram portal** he sees the match and approves the conversation, or declines it. From that moment he reads it from the very beginning.
3. **Both of them know.** The conversation says plainly that her mahram is reading it, so nobody writes thinking they are alone.

If all of her mahrams decline a conversation, it stays locked. The same happens if something stands between the mahram and one of the two, such as a block: the conversation does not continue without oversight until another verified mahram approves it, the block is lifted, or she turns the feature off.

## Reading is not writing

Following a conversation does not mean taking part in it. From the portal the mahram reads. If he wants to write in a particular conversation, he asks for permission, and she or the person she is talking to decides, whoever answers first. If the request is refused, he can ask again after seven days. Permission is given for that one conversation, and it can be taken back.

The portal is only for looking after her. A mahram does not use it to meet anyone.

## When it is time for the families to speak

At some point the conversation in the app has done its job. Either of the two can then tap **"Take this further"**. The other side does not see it: nothing happens until both have said it, and until then each of them can withdraw.

When both have said it, the families are introduced. Each side's mahram receives the other side's name and contact by email, so they can speak outside the app, the way this has always been done. Someone who has no mahram on Niyyah can still say it; then they receive the other side's contact themselves, and the other side receives theirs.

## Why it is built this way

The app does not replace the family, and it does not claim to. What it can do is make sure the conversation is never hidden from the people whose opinion matters, and that the step from the app to the families is a short one. Whether this is the right path for you is a question for you, your family and, if you are unsure, an imam you trust. More in the [mahram section of the Niyyah page](/en#mahrem).
